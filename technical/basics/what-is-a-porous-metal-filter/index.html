What Is a Porous Metal Filter?

2026-09-05  · 3 min read

The term porous metal filter describes any filtration component whose separating function comes from void space engineered into a metal body. Rather than filtering on a woven or fibrous surface, a porous metal filter forces fluid through a three-dimensional maze of interconnected pores inside the metal itself — and captures particles throughout that volume.

“Sintered metal filter” and “porous metal filter” overlap heavily: most porous metal filters in industry are made by powder sintering, and most sintered metal filters are porous. The useful distinction is that porous metal emphasizes the structure (a metal with designed voids), while sintered emphasizes the manufacturing route.

What “Porous Metal” Means

A porous metal is a metal component containing a controlled volume fraction of open, connected pores. Two parameters describe it:

  • Porosity — the fraction of the bulk volume that is void, usually expressed as a percentage. Powder-sintered filter media typically balance enough porosity for useful flow against enough metal for mechanical strength.
  • Pore size and connectivity — how large the individual voids and pore throats are, and whether they form continuous passages. Filtration requires open porosity: pores that connect through the wall so fluid can pass. Closed pores — bubbles sealed inside the metal — contribute nothing to flow.

Porosity is not an independent dial: raising porosity increases permeability but reduces strength and dirt capacity. Lowering it does the opposite. Every porous metal product sits at a deliberate compromise between these properties, chosen for its duty.

How Porous Metal Filters Compare to Other Porous Media

Engineers typically choose among three families of porous media. Porous metal occupies the high-strength, high-temperature corner:

PropertyPorous metalPorous polymerPorous ceramic
Mechanical strengthHigh — load-bearingLow — needs supportModerate — brittle
Temperature limitHigh (alloy-dependent)Low — polymer-limitedVery high
Chemical resistanceBroad (steel/titanium)Limited by polymerVery broad
Cleanability / reuseYes — backwash, ultrasonic, chemicalLimitedYes, but fragile
Impact / vibration toleranceExcellentPoorPoor
Relative costHigherLowModerate

Porous polymers (sintered PE, PTFE, PP) are inexpensive and chemically versatile at ambient conditions, but they soften with temperature, swell in some solvents, and cannot be steam- cleaned at metal-media temperatures. Porous ceramics handle extreme temperature and corrosion but are brittle — a knock, a thermal shock, or an over-tightened gasket can crack them.

Porous metal filters accept a level of mechanical abuse that would end either alternative: pressure pulses, vibration, thermal shock within material limits, and repeated cleaning cycles. Where the process is hostile in several ways at once — hot, corrosive, and mechanically demanding — porous metal is often the only media class that survives.

Forms of Porous Metal Filters

The same porous metal technology appears in several component forms:

  • Tubular filter elements — the standard cartridge form for process filtration.
  • Filter discs and plates — flat porous components for compact housings.
  • Spargers and aeration elements — porous metal used in reverse, dispersing gas into liquids through its pore network.
  • Silencers, flame arrestors, and flow restrictors — non-filtration uses of the same engineered porosity.

On this site we focus on the first three: filter elements, spargers, and filter discs, in sintered stainless steel and titanium.

When to Specify a Porous Metal Filter

A porous metal filter earns its place when one or more of these is true:

  1. The process temperature exceeds what polymer media tolerate.
  2. The fluid chemically attacks polymer media (aggressive solvents, strong oxidizers, chloride-bearing streams).
  3. The duty involves pressure pulses, vibration, or thermal cycling that fatigue fragile media.
  4. The element must be cleaned and reused many times for economic or regulatory reasons.
  5. The filter must be welded, machined, or otherwise integrated as a metal component.

If none of these apply, a simpler medium may be more economical — porous metal is a specialist’s choice, not a default.

Summary

A porous metal filter is a metal component with deliberately engineered open porosity that separates particles from fluids while tolerating heat, chemistry, and mechanical stress that destroy other media.

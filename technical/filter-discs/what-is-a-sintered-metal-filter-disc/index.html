What Is a Sintered Metal Filter Disc?

2026-09-17  · 3 min read

A sintered metal filter disc is a flat, rigid, porous metal component made by sintering metal powder into disc or plate form. It packs the same all-metal filtration technology as a tubular sintered element into a thin flat geometry — a self-supporting filter medium that drops into compact housings, instrument ports, and analytical devices.

Depending on diameter and thickness, the industry calls these components discs, plates, or sheets; the names describe geometry ranges rather than different products. What defines the component is its sintered porous structure, and that is identical in kind to every other powder sintered filter.

Construction

A sintered metal filter disc is built by the standard powder metallurgy route, shaped flat:

  1. Powder selection — stainless steel or titanium powder with a controlled particle size distribution; particle size sets the achievable pore structure.
  2. Forming — the powder is filled or pressed into a flat mold at the target diameter and thickness. Thinner discs flow more freely; thicker plates trade permeability for mechanical capacity.
  3. Sintering — the compact is heated under controlled atmosphere below the metal’s melting point. Particles bond at contact points into a rigid plate while open, connected pores remain through the thickness.
  4. Finishing — edges are prepared for the sealing concept: plain edges for clamping between flanges or gaskets, machined rims, or welded carriers.

The finished disc is monolithic — there is no seam, no media layer, no adhesive. The filter medium and its support are one piece of porous metal.

How It Filters

Fluid approaches one face and passes through the full thickness of the porous body. Because the tortuous pore network occupies the entire volume, particles are captured throughout the disc — depth filtration — not merely at the upstream face. This gives a flat disc real dirt-holding capacity and forgiving pressure-drop behavior, properties it shares with tubular sintered elements and lacks compared with wire mesh screens, which stop particles at a surface.

Retention is specified as a filtration rating tied to the pore structure, with porosity, permeability, and bubble point as the measurable companions. As with all sintered products, exact values depend on powder and sintering conditions and should be taken from the supplier’s verified data for the specific disc.

Why Choose a Sintered Disc?

Against disposable discs (paper, polymer, membrane), the sintered metal disc wins wherever the duty exceeds plastic and paper: temperature, aggressive chemistry, pressure differential, erosive particles, or simply the economics of a disc that is cleaned and reused for years instead of replaced every cycle. Against wire mesh discs, it wins on depth filtration, finer available ratings, and freedom from weave damage.

Its practical limits are equally clear: a rigid disc cannot be pleated, so filtration area per housing volume is modest; and the all-metal premium only pays back where the duty is genuinely hostile or reuse cycles are numerous.

Typical Applications

  • Compact inline filtration — disc filters clamped between flange faces on process lines, sampling systems, and small-bore piping.
  • Instrument and sensor protection — porous discs shield transmitters, analyzers, and control orifices from particles while passing the measured fluid.
  • Analytical and laboratory devices — filtration, diffusion, and support discs in analytical equipment and pilot rigs.
  • Catalyst and bed support — discs holding catalyst beds, packing, or membranes in place while passing flow.
  • Steam and sterile service — points of use where only metal survives the temperature.

Materials

Sintered filter discs are available in the two PMFIL material platforms: stainless steel (316L-class grades for broad process duty) and titanium (chloride and corrosive service at half the weight). Custom diameters and thicknesses may be available upon request.

Summary

A sintered metal filter disc is the flat member of the powder sintered filter family: a rigid, cleanable, depth-filtration medium in miniature, built for duties where disposable discs melt, tear, or wear out.

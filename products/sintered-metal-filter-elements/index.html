Product Introduction

Sintered metal filter elements are rigid, self-supporting filter components made by sintering metal powder — most commonly stainless steel or titanium — into a porous body with a controlled pore structure. Because the entire element is one continuous porous metal part, there is no filter media to pleat, wrap, or glue: the element itself is the medium.

Construction

A powder sintered filter element starts as metal powder. The powder is compacted or filled into a mold in the shape of the finished element — typically a tube or cylinder — and then heated in a sintering furnace to a temperature below the melting point of the metal. At sintering temperature, atomic diffusion forms bonds at the contact points between particles. The particles fuse into a rigid, interconnected matrix while the voids between them remain open as a continuous network of flow channels.

The key structural features of the finished element are:

  • Porous filter body — the sintered powder matrix that provides the actual filtration and mechanical support in one piece.
  • Pore structure — the size, shape, and connectivity of the voids between particles, which determines how fluid flows through the element and what it retains.
  • Connection / support structure — depending on the design, the element may be sintered with integral end fittings, welded to collars or threads, or mounted in a housing. Connection styles are application-specific.

Because porosity is created by the spaces between powder particles, pore geometry is closely related to powder particle size and sintering conditions. Finer powders and controlled sintering produce finer pores; this is the mechanism behind the graded filtration ratings available in powder sintered elements.
